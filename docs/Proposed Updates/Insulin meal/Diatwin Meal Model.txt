Part 1 — Currently Used / Approved Reference Models
The UVA/Padova T1D Simulator family is the gold standard in this space. There are three FDA-accepted versions:
S2008 (Original) — the first computer model accepted by FDA as a substitute for preclinical trials of certain insulin treatments, including closed-loop algorithms. It includes a population of 300 in silico subjects (100 adults, 100 adolescents, 100 children) PubMed Central covering the GI tract, glucose kinetics, and insulin kinetics subsystems.
S2013 (Updated) — incorporates a model of counterregulation and a new description of glucose dynamics in hypoglycemia PubMed Central, and was separately accepted by FDA. It added nonlinear hypoglycemic response and an alpha-cell glucagon model.
S2017 (Single-Day Extension) — intraday variability of insulin sensitivity (SI) has been modeled, based on clinical T1D data, accounting for both intra- and intersubject variability of daily SI, thus extending the domain from "single-meal" to "single-day" scenarios. PubMed Central It also added the dawn phenomenon model, fast-acting insulin analog PK, intradermal and inhaled insulin models.
S2017 + Macronutrient Extension (2025, research) — the UVA/Padova T1D simulator was updated by integrating fat and protein effects on model parameters to allow realistic simulation of meals with varying content ScienceDirect, though the updated simulator still lacks proper validation against data free from confounding factors ScienceDirect.
These are the primary approved benchmarks. The Dalla Man et al. (2006) GI subsystem paper underpins all versions.

Part 2 — NATIVE Model Alignment Assessment
Here is a systematic assessment of your model against the S2008/S2013/S2017 lineage, organized by subsystem:

✅ Strong Alignment
Gamma-shaped Rate of Appearance (Ra)
Your meal-pharmacodynamics.ts gamma PDF integration is conceptually the closest available analytic approximation to the ODE-derived Ra(t) in Dalla Man 2006. The curve shapes are functionally equivalent for standard-sized meals, which is your most important structural alignment point.
Stacked meal superposition
Both NATIVE's calculateStackedMealEffect and the UVA/Padova ODE system handle multiple concurrent meals by linear superposition — physiologically correct when glucose transporters are not saturated.
Linear BG sensitivity factor
Your carb_to_bg_factor (4.0 mg/dL/g) is a valid linear-regime approximation of UVA/Padova's tissue-level Vmx×G_t/(Km+G_t) term. For moderate meals, this holds well.
Protein & fat modeling — NATIVE is ahead of the base model
The base S2008/S2013/S2017 simulators are CHO-only. A macronutrient extension integrating fat and protein effects is a recent 2025 research update that still lacks full validation ScienceDirect. Your protein_factor = 0.5 and fat_factor = 0.2 carb-equivalent approach is therefore a clinically motivated extension that is ahead of the approved baseline, not a gap. This is a platform differentiator worth documenting and validating.
Safety classification
UVA/Padova leaves threshold-based alerts to the controller layer — your rule-based safety classifier is an appropriate application-level addition with no contradicting reference.

⚠️ Partial Alignment (Functional but Simplified)
IOB vs. remote insulin compartment
Your IOB fraction empirically captures the insulin effect delay, but UVA/Padova's remote compartment X(t) with rate constant p2U is the physiologically grounded mechanism. For standard meal scenarios the two converge reasonably well; the divergence appears most in rapid insulin stacking events and in the hypoglycemic tail.
5 discrete absorption profiles vs. continuous k_empt
Your absorption type system (very_fast → very_slow) captures the qualitative spectrum of GI kinetics. The alignment gap is dose-dependency: UVA/Padova's nonlinear sigmoid k_empt slows gastric emptying for larger carb loads (>50 g CHO), which your flat modifiers do not reproduce. For typical meals (20–50 g CHO) the difference is modest; for large meals (>80 g CHO) your model will overestimate early BG rise.
Meal timing factors vs. S2017 circadian SI
S2017 provides time-varying distributions of both basal insulin infusion and insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio to capture intraday variability of insulin sensitivity PubMed. Your static meal_timing_factors capture the same intent but as fixed scalars rather than patient-fitted circadian functions — a reasonable approximation for a clinical decision-support context.

❌ Structural Gaps (Requiring Targeted Work)
Single BG value vs. two-compartment glucose kinetics
UVA/Padova separates plasma glucose (G_p) and tissue glucose (G_t) with explicit transfer rates k1/k2. This split is what produces the realistic post-meal overshoot and the tissue-uptake delay. Your single estimatedBG value cannot reproduce this pattern. This is the highest-impact gap for mixed-composition meal accuracy.
No EGP model
Endogenous glucose production (hepatic suppression by portal insulin) is absent from NATIVE. Your daily-reset baseline partially compensates at a population level, but individual overnight and fasting excursions will be systematically mismodeled. A minimal-viable approximation — a basal_egp constant reduced by IOB fraction — would close most of this gap without a full ODE.
Cumulative bank-balance vs. continuous ODE state
This is NATIVE's most architecturally distinct choice. The persist-at-100% mechanism and daily reset have no analogue in UVA/Padova. This is appropriate for a clinical app context (interpretable state, no ODE solver required), but it means your model diverges progressively over a multi-meal day when meal and insulin effects from the morning carry forward in a way that the UVA/Padova continuous state does not. This needs explicit clinical validation rather than physiological derivation.
